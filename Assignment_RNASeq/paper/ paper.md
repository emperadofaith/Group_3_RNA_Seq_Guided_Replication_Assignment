
**RNA-Seq Guided Replication Using Galaxy and Github**

**INTRODUCTION**

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) is a molecular method that analyzes RNA transcripts generated by cells under particular biological conditions to investigate gene expression. It gives details on the genes that are actively expressed as well as how their expression varies depending on the situation. Interpreting these changes requires an understanding of how the genome, genes, RNA, and gene expression are related.

A published RNA-seq study on Salmonella typhi infection of human intestinal tissue was chosen for examination in this exercise. The pathogen's interaction with the host's mucosal immune defenses and transcriptional alterations linked to early infection were investigated. The study's publicly accessible RNA-seq data were utilized to analyze gene expression patterns, biological replicates, and experimental conditions.

Galaxy was used to perform quality control, read mapping, gene counting, and differential expression analysis on the RNA-seq data. The results of the initial study were then compared with the resulting gene expression patterns. This exercise shows how reproducible molecular biology research can be supported by reanalyzing and documenting publicly available sequencing data using GitHub and Galaxy.


**OBJECTIVES**

Understand the relationship between genomes, genes, RNA, and gene expression.

Examine a published RNA-seq study and its research design.

Locate and access the study’s RNA-seq data.

Identify experimental groups, controls, treatments, and replicates.

Perform RNA-seq quality control, read mapping, and gene counting.

Compare gene expression between biological conditions.

Identify upregulated and downregulated genes.

Compare our results with the original study.

Document the analysis using Galaxy and GitHub.
